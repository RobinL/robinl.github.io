# Data science in the age of AI



Data science has always been about applying the best tools and technology maximise the value of data.  I’ve been in the profession for over a decade, and in retrospect it’s surprising how little changed over most of that period.  The tools evolved, but ultimately my day-to-day work looked similar in 2013 and 2023: I spent most of my time programming in Python.



The rise of LLMs has been the biggest shakeup to data science that I have experienced.  The aim has not changed: we still seek to turn data into insights and recommendations, and ultimately to influence decisions.  But the work \- in terms of the activities I’m doing day to day \- has changed radically.

I used to spend most of my time programming, but in the last year I’ve barely written a single line of code. Instead, I spend most of my time interacting with AI agents.

Paraphrasing Matt Rocklin:

> *Our job is now to construct feedback systems that guide AI to good solutions, not to write code ourselves.*
> [*Source*](https://mrocklin.spicytakes.org/)
>
I find this in equal parts worrying and exciting. But stepping back, programming was always the means rather than the end. The value of data scientists remains in our creativity and judgement: understanding business problems, finding ways to use data to solve them, and working iteratively with our customers towards better solutions.

In the remainder of this article, I look at what this new world looks like in practice. I use a series of real world examples that bring to life what it means to guide agents to good solutions, and the most effective ways I have found to use them.
